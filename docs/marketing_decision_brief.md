## Conditions, reversal and no-send

- Finance confirms the contribution margin on incremental sales and CRM confirms the full cost per e-mail (delivery, offer, handling); the cost must be below margin x 0.77 USD, and below margin x 0.49 USD to be safe on the lower bound.
- E-mail consent and unsubscribe handling are verified for every recipient.
- A 10% randomized no-e-mail holdout is drawn and frozen before sending (at least 3,063 customers per arm to detect an effect of the 2008 size with 80% power).
- A named human approves the final draft; any change to the plan requires a new review.
- The 2008 effect is a prior, not a forecast: the first send is itself the pilot and is judged on its own holdout.

Would reverse the decision:
- Verified cost per e-mail above the break-even for the verified margin (point: margin x 0.77 USD): choose no e-mail.
- Consent cannot be verified, or unsubscribe and complaint costs are material: choose no e-mail or a smaller consented group.
- A new randomized pilot on the current base gives an interval for additional buyers that includes zero: stop and research before spending more.
- A larger test shows the model beating random and the simple rule with intervals above zero: switch to model-targeted sending.
- A binding contact cap (budget or frequency policy): prioritise by a simple auditable rule and randomize inside the cap, as described in the alternative.

**No e-mail.** Always allowed. Without an e-mail 0.57% of customers still bought within two weeks; those purchases are not caused by the campaign. Choose no e-mail when cost per contact exceeds break-even, when consent is missing, or when a manager prefers to wait for a current pilot. Under the conservative assumption set the Womens e-mail loses money (-2,871 USD scenario contribution).

**If only a share can be contacted.** If only a share of customers may be contacted, do not present the model as a proven gain. At a 25% cap the model (10.0), the recency rule (8.6) and the past-spend rule (11.6) all sit above random (7.4) on the point estimate, none with an interval that excludes zero. Use the simplest auditable ranking (past-year spend or recency) to fill the cap, and randomize e-mail versus no e-mail inside both the selected and the unselected group, so the next campaign measures whether targeting works.
